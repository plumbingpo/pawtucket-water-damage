Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Pawtucket

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. What matters most is what was in it. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

The first thing our Pawtucket team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Coverage

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A worn wax ring that lets water out at the base each time the toilet is flushed.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • A cracked tank or failed supply line that floods the bathroom with clean water.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Lingering Odor

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

Subfloor Saturation

Through the grout and around the toilet base, water works its way below tile or vinyl flooring. Once wet, the plywood subfloor swells, its layers separate, and the floor covering bonded on top can come loose.

Bacterial Contamination

Water from the drain or bowl deposits bacteria in drywall, baseboards, carpet and pad. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

2

We extract standing water, pull contaminated carpet pad, baseboards or drywall where needed, and lift the toilet if water has gone under the base and flange.

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.
