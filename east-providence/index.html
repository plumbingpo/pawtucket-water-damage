How East Providence Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in East Providence homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Start with supply lines. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. Typical Water Damage Problems in East Providence

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. Our crews in East Providence follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in East Providence, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. Our Pawtucket Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear.
